### Recommendation: name GUIDE and SCAN, with different, conditional routes

**GUIDE: yes, as an ask-your-doctor referral, not a FirstLight offering.** CMS describes a coordinated dementia-care model with caregiver education/support and respite for qualifying patients/caregivers. A clinician on the participating team must confirm dementia. The person needs Original Medicare Parts A and B as primary payer, not Medicare Advantage or PACE; must meet residence rules; cannot have elected Medicare hospice or already be aligned elsewhere. People in long-term nursing homes or memory care units are not eligible. Some approved residential-care settings have additional partnership rules. A doctor can help find a participating team; the team assesses, obtains consent and sends information to CMS for confirmation. No dollar amounts were added. [CMS model](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/guide) [CMS eligibility/access FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/guide/faqs)

**FirstLight participation remains unconfirmed, not disproven.** I downloaded and searched every cell of the CMS participant workbook, which says "Last updated October 2026." No `FirstLight`, `First Light` or `MINGA` name match was found. That is not proof that no FirstLight entity participates under a different legal name or as a contracted partner. CMS permits non-Medicare-enrolled partner organizations, and participants maintain partner rosters that are not comprehensively identified by this public participant list. The page therefore says "No FirstLight participation is confirmed here," not "FirstLight does not participate." [CMS participant workbook](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/guide-participant-list.xlsx) [CMS partner explanation](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/guide/faqs)

**SCAN: yes, only as a plan-specific question for existing members.** It is a Medicare Advantage health plan, not a universal home-care grant. Its official 2026 at-home-support material describes plan-dependent daily-living assistance and caregiver respite. The member must check their Evidence of Coverage and Member Services, including authorized providers and limits. We do not recommend enrolling/changing plans or imply FirstLight network participation. Because GUIDE excludes Medicare Advantage enrollment, the page explicitly says GUIDE and SCAN are different routes that cannot be combined. HICAP provides a neutral counseling route before coverage changes. [SCAN 2026 at-home support](https://www.scanhealthplan.com/en/scan-members/member-benefit-materials-and-information/2026-supplemental-benefits/at-home-support) [CMS eligibility](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/guide/faqs) [California HICAP](https://aging.ca.gov/Programs_and_Services/Medicare_Counseling/)

## Quiz and retained program rules

Eight optional questions, with Back/Next/Skip and progress:

1. Who are you finding help for? Includes "Two people in the same home, such as a couple."
2. What everyday help would be useful?
3. What best describes their memory needs?
4. Is this after a hospital stay or surgery?
5. When might help be needed?
6. Is the person a Veteran or a surviving spouse?
7. Do they have long-term care insurance?
8. How are they thinking about paying for care?

- Company/meals/household tasks suggest companion care; bathing/dressing/toileting suggest personal care; both suggest both.
- Memory concerns or a diagnosis suggest dementia/Alzheimer's care, without diagnosing.
- Recovery after hospital/surgery suggests transitional care.
- "Give our family caregiver a break" remains within the scheduling question and suggests respite care. Overnight/24-hour selections add scheduling questions, not a separate care type.
- Veteran/surviving-spouse answers suggest Veteran care and questions about VA pension-related Aid & Attendance. Selecting Veterans benefits as a payment approach also raises that benefits-review topic. Requirements need a separate VA review; the quiz does not establish eligibility. [VA source](https://www.va.gov/pension/aid-attendance-housebound/)
- A Veteran plus daily-living help, memory concerns or caregiver-break support raises the existing VA home-care/community-care topic. The wording directs people to a VA social worker and says enrollment, assessed need, availability, eligibility and a VA-contracted provider matter. It does not say a FirstLight office accepts VA payment. [VA source](https://www.va.gov/geriatrics/pages/Homemaker_and_Home_Health_Aide_Care.asp)
- An existing long-term care policy, or choosing insurance payment, suggests insurer/policy review. Coverage, limits, waiting periods and provider requirements need confirmation. [California Department of Aging source](https://aging.ca.gov/Providers_and_Partners/Health_Insurance_Counseling_and_Advocacy_Program/Long_Term_Care_Insurance/)
- Income/savings/assets, family paying, home equity/sale, waiting for funding, exploring options, or "not sure" produce: "Financial consulting may be available to help you explore ways to pay for care." The next line asks whether a free consultation is available locally, rather than promising one. Insurance-only, VA-only and skipped payment answers do not independently trigger it.
- Summary copy states this is a guide, not an assessment or eligibility decision. New official-source referrals are listed above. No program approval is inferred, and completely skipped answers still produce no program-specific recommendation.
