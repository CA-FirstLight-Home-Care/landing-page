Understand the options

Different kinds of help. 1 plan for your life.

You don’t need to know the right service name. Start with the tasks that are getting harder. A plan can combine more than 1 kind of support.

Companion care = company + everyday help

Meals, light household tasks, errands and someone to spend time with.

Personal care = hands-on daily support

Help with bathing, dressing, toileting, eating and moving around.

Compare 7 types of non-medical home care
Care type What it is Who it is for Typical tasks Typical schedule
Companion care What it is

Practical and social support. If bathing, dressing or toileting requires hands-on help, include personal care in the plan.

Who it is for

For someone who wants company and help keeping everyday life on track.

Typical tasks

Conversation, shared activities, meal preparation, light housekeeping, errands, transportation and medication reminders.

Typical schedule

Part-time or full-time support, with visits agreed with your office.

Personal care What it is

Hands-on support, rather than companionship alone. It is not a replacement for nursing or medical treatment.

Who it is for

For someone who needs hands-on help with daily personal tasks.

Typical tasks

Help with bathing, dressing, grooming, eating, toileting, walking and getting in or out of a bed or chair, as agreed in the care plan.

Typical schedule

Visits planned around the daily routines where help is needed.

Dementia & Alzheimer’s care What it is

Companion and personal support adapted to the person’s memory, preferences and routines. It does not diagnose or treat dementia.

Who it is for

For a person living with dementia, and the family supporting them.

Typical tasks

A personalized routine, familiar activities, reassurance, meals, reminders and help with personal tasks when needed.

Typical schedule

Part-time, full-time or broader coverage, subject to a safe plan and local staffing.

Respite care What it is

The purpose is to give the family caregiver a break. It can include several kinds of care; it is not a separate medical service.

Who it is for

For a family caregiver who needs time to rest, work or be away.

Typical tasks

A caregiver steps in to provide the companion or personal support your loved one needs while you take a break.

Typical schedule

A planned break or temporary coverage; visit length and minimums vary by office.

Post-surgery & transitional care What it is

Everyday help during recovery or a move between care settings. Nursing, wound care and therapy require the appropriate licensed provider.

Who it is for

For an adult returning home after a hospital stay, surgery, illness or injury.

Typical tasks

Help settling into a daily routine: meals, light household tasks, mobility, transportation to follow-ups and medication reminders.

Typical schedule

Hours and duration are based on recovery needs and reviewed with the local team.

Veteran care What it is

The care can be the same daily support offered to other adults. Eligible veterans may have benefit options; approval is not automatic.

Who it is for

For veterans and families who need support with everyday living.

Typical tasks

Companion and personal care, including meals, household tasks, bathing, dressing and mobility support.

Typical schedule

Based on assessed needs, office availability and any benefit authorization.

Couples care What it is

1 caregiver supports 2 people in the same home when their individual needs can be safely met together.

Who it is for

2 adults in the same home, such as a couple, who each need everyday support.

Typical tasks

Agreed companion or personal-care tasks for each person. Each person needs a separate assessment; 1 caregiver may not be enough for simultaneous hands-on needs.

Typical schedule

Visits agreed with the local office after assessing both people. Rates vary by local office.

Overnight, 24-hour
& live-in questions

Some people need a few hours of help; others need support across the day and night. FirstLight describes options up to 24/7 care and live-in services, but availability and arrangements vary by office.

The plan may combine the kinds of care above. Ask your local team exactly who will be there, which hours are covered, and how overnight needs, rest periods and backup care are handled. Do not assume “live-in” and continuous awake care mean the same thing.

This page describes non-medical care. Services, safe task limits and schedules must be confirmed with your local office. Caregivers do not replace your doctor, nurse or therapist.
